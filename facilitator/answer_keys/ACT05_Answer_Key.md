# ACT05 Answer Key: GIS Decision Support

## Expected procedure

Participants assemble verified facts from QGIS, state the analysis performed, identify limitations, and then prompt AI. They mark every sentence as supported, requires correction, or unsupported.

## Expected result

An acceptable report uses four labeled parts:

**Observation:** states what the mapped overlay shows.

**Interpretation:** explains what the pattern may indicate without claiming causation or certainty.

**Limitation:** states what the data and method do not establish.

**Possible Action:** recommends validation or consideration by the responsible office, not an automatic government decision.

## Verification

Every number must trace to an attribute table, selection count, or saved result. Every geographic name must exist in the dataset. Remove forecast timing, policy thresholds, and causal claims that were not supplied.

## Common mistakes

Reporting synthetic values as official; calling intersection actual flooding or damage; omitting model limitations; treating an AI-generated priority as an order; adding “immediately” without operational authority.

## Human validation

The GIS analyst validates spatial facts. The domain office interprets operational relevance. The authorized official makes or approves the decision.

## Acceptable alternatives

A one-page executive brief, situation-report section, or map narrative is acceptable if it preserves the four evidence layers and traceability.

## Discussion questions

Which sentence is observation and which is interpretation? What additional evidence would justify a stronger action? Who must approve the final wording?

## Evidence of understanding

The participant catches an introduced claim, states a meaningful limitation, and keeps possible action proportional to the evidence.
