# ACT05 Workflow: AI-Assisted GIS Decision Support

**Official slot:** Day 3, 10:15 AM to 11:15 AM

**Required output:** AI-assisted GIS report

## Objective

Turn verified spatial evidence into a concise decision-support narrative while preserving the boundary between map observation, interpretation, limitation, possible action, and official decision.

## Scenario

An LGU department head needs a short explanation of the training flood-exposure analysis. Participants may give AI only facts that they verified in QGIS.

## Required files

`outputs/screenshots/ACT05_01_Decision_Support_Map.png`, the ACT05 prompt, and the participant’s verified results.

## Timed procedure

1. **Evidence versus decision, 8 minutes:** Separate GIS result from official action.
2. **Report demonstration, 10 minutes:** Provide AI with verified facts and explicit prohibitions.
3. **Group drafting, 14 minutes:** Produce Observation, Interpretation, Limitation, and Possible Action.
4. **Verification checklist, 10 minutes:** Trace each number, place, and claim to a supplied fact.
5. **Smart City synthesis, 13 minutes:** Complete the problem, decision, GIS, AI, verification, responsibility, and benefit fields.
6. **Closing, 5 minutes:** Repeat the accountability statement.

## Expected result

An AI-assisted GIS report that uses only verified facts, preserves uncertainty, states limitations, and leaves the official decision with the authorized LGU office.

## Verification

Compare every number and geographic statement with the QGIS output. Mark any statement that AI introduced. Confirm that possible actions remain proportionate to the evidence.

## Common mistakes

Calling mapped exposure actual damage; turning a model into a forecast; adding unsupported causes; presenting a review level as an evacuation order; omitting source limitations; using synthetic values in an official report.

## Human validation

The GIS analyst confirms the map facts, the domain office interprets operational relevance, and the authorized official makes the decision.
